# Response to Reviewer VkVU (rating 2, reject)

Thank you for the review. The two stated concerns are novelty and end-to-end impact; we address both, plus the theory question, and would appreciate the reviewer weighing the new lower bound and the corrected framing when reconsidering the score.

## On novelty: the components are standard; the contributions are not the components

We agree, and state in the paper, that Platt scaling, logistic fusion, isotonic/PAV, Venn–Abers, and Mondrian stratification are all prior art. The paper's claimed contributions are elsewhere:

1. **A new theorem.** Theorem 1 is, to our knowledge, the first lower bound showing worst-cell sample complexity Θ(α⁻² log K) is *unavoidable* for the entire class F_n of post-hoc calibrators that touch calibration data only through per-cell empirical means — matching the HKRR upper bound and thereby closing the gap for that class. This is new theory, not an application of existing results; the proof is in the released `theory/proofs.tex` together with a preservation theorem (multicalibration of the fused predictor under conditional independence, which prior work established only for marginal calibration) and the per-stratum Mondrian–Venn–Abers validity statement.
2. **A new measurement.** The marginal-vs-worst-subgroup calibration gap in retrieval *fusion* had not been documented: every standard fusion pipeline we test hides worst-subgroup miscalibration 1.4–7.7× its marginal ECE (e.g. RRF on fiqa: 0.011 marginal vs 0.086 worst-subgroup ECE-15), which means the standard monitoring practice for thresholded RAG is systematically blind. Multicalibration has, to our knowledge, not previously been applied to multi-signal retrieval fusion; identifying signal-dominance strata as the subgroup family that is simultaneously label-free at inference, deployment-computable, and empirically where the failures concentrate is part of the contribution.
3. **The assembly is theorem-directed, not a grab-bag.** The specific composition — calibrate signals, fuse parametrically, then stratified *monotone nonparametric* calibration rather than per-cell correction — is chosen precisely to exit the class that Theorem 1 proves is sample-starved. Components being individually standard is the norm for calibration papers (Platt, temperature scaling, HKRR are all compositions of standard pieces); we ask that the combination be judged by whether it is principled and whether it wins. During the discussion period we rescaled the core comparison to 20 reseeded splits (4× the paper's protocol, from the released frozen matrices): CalFuse has the lowest worst-subgroup ECE-15 on 6 of 7 subsets and is statistically tied for lowest on the seventh; vs the strongest learned baseline it takes three wins at p≤0.002 (nfcorpus and fiqa at p<0.0001, d≈2.4–3.1), four ties, no losses.

## Q1. "Is there a direct theoretical validation of CalFuse itself?"

Partly, and we will make the boundary explicit rather than implied. CalFuse carries two direct guarantees: (i) distribution-free finite-sample per-stratum coverage of its Venn–Abers envelope under exchangeability (Mondrian–Venn–Abers validity), and (ii) preservation of multicalibration by the parametric fusion stage under conditional independence. What it does not carry is a sample-complexity optimality theorem; Theorem 1 characterizes the class CalFuse *leaves* (showing that class is maximally sample-hungry in the worst cell), not the estimator itself. A matching upper bound for stratified monotone calibration is open, and the revision states this in Limitations.

The connection is, however, not merely rhetorical — it is testable, and we tested it during the discussion period: our HKRR-style per-cell correction baseline (`calfuse_multical`) is an empirical instantiation of exactly the class F_n that Theorem 1 lower-bounds, and on worst-subgroup ECE-15 it loses to CalFuse on 3 of 7 subsets (p<0.05, 5-seed paired t) and never wins. The theorem predicts per-cell audits are worst-cell sample-starved at these calibration sizes; the data agrees. The revision will add a "What Theorem 1 does and does not say" paragraph plus this empirical confirmation so no reader infers more — or less — than the theory delivers.

## Q2. "Full-coverage NDCG is unchanged, so where is the end-to-end gain?"

Unchanged full-coverage NDCG is the *expected* outcome, not a shortfall: post-hoc calibration is near-monotone on the fused score, so it cannot and should not reorder rankings — a method that improved NDCG here would be doing something other than calibration. The claimed deployment value sits at the decision layer of a thresholded system, and is demonstrated: (a) τ acquires probability semantics that hold per-subgroup — a new threshold-reliability experiment (5 reseeded splits × 7 subsets, from the released frozen matrices) makes this concrete: at fixed τ, the worst-stratum gap between realized precision and stated probability shrinks from 0.097 (Linear-Learned) to 0.036 (CalFuse) on nfcorpus (paired p<0.001), 0.151 to 0.046 on scidocs (p=0.010), significant on 4 of 7 subsets and never significantly worse, while RRF's realized precision at the same τ varies by up to 0.78 across strata; (b) selective operation improves — on scifact, abstaining on the bottom half of CalFuse-P's calibrated scores cuts hallucination-among-answered from 30.8% to 15.2% (5-seed means), a coverage/factuality trade-off a miscalibrated score cannot expose with known semantics; (c) at full coverage, an end-to-end hallucination reduction of −3.49 pp vs the strongest learned baseline on scifact, strengthened during the discussion period to 10 seeds (p=0.0025, d=−1.31), with a mechanism test attributing it to distractor swap rather than ranking change. We are transparent about scope: the effect is subset-dependent at 7B (one robust win, one parametric-base deficit that the full method repairs to parity, three inconclusive — full table in the response to Reviewer utWJ) and vanishes at Qwen-14B, where all fusion methods converge. The revision scopes the claim accordingly: calibration is not a ranking improvement and does not claim to be; it makes the *thresholding* of retrieval trustworthy, uniformly over subgroups, and reaches end-to-end generation only where the generator is sensitive to context composition.

If the reviewer's remaining concern is a specific missing baseline or a specific experiment that would make the novelty judgment concrete, we would welcome the pointer and will run it within the discussion period where feasible on the frozen score matrices.
