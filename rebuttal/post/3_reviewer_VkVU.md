Thank you for the review. Your two concerns are novelty and end-to-end impact; we address both directly, with new experiments run during the discussion period.

**On novelty: the components are standard; the contributions are not the components.**

We agree — and state — that Platt scaling, logistic fusion, isotonic/PAV, Venn–Abers, and Mondrian stratification are prior art. The claimed contributions are:

1. **A new theorem.** Theorem 1 is, to our knowledge, the first lower bound showing worst-cell sample complexity Θ(α⁻² log K) is unavoidable for the entire class F_n of post-hoc calibrators that touch calibration data only through per-cell empirical means — matching the HKRR upper bound and closing the gap for that class. This is new theory, not an application of existing results.
2. **A new measurement.** The marginal-vs-worst-subgroup calibration gap in retrieval *fusion* had not been documented: every standard fusion pipeline we test hides worst-subgroup miscalibration 1.4–7.7× its marginal ECE (e.g. RRF on fiqa: 0.011 marginal vs 0.086 worst-subgroup), so standard monitoring for thresholded RAG is systematically blind. Multicalibration has not previously been applied to multi-signal retrieval fusion; identifying signal-dominance strata as the subgroup family that is label-free at inference, deployment-computable, and where failures concentrate is part of the contribution.
3. **A theorem-directed assembly.** The composition — calibrate signals, fuse parametrically, then stratified *monotone nonparametric* calibration rather than per-cell correction — is chosen precisely to exit the class Theorem 1 proves sample-starved. Standard components are the norm in calibration (Platt, temperature scaling, HKRR are all such compositions); we ask that the combination be judged on principle and results. At 20 reseeded splits (4× the submitted protocol): CalFuse has the lowest worst-subgroup ECE-15 on 6 of 7 subsets, statistically tied for lowest on the seventh; vs the strongest learned baseline, three wins at p≤0.002 (nfcorpus, fiqa at p<0.0001, d≈2.4–3.1), four ties, no losses.

**Q1. Direct theoretical validation of CalFuse itself?**

Partly, and the revision makes the boundary explicit. CalFuse carries two direct guarantees: (i) distribution-free finite-sample per-stratum coverage of its Venn–Abers envelope under exchangeability; (ii) multicalibration preservation of the parametric fusion stage under conditional independence. It does not carry a sample-complexity optimality theorem: Theorem 1 characterizes the class CalFuse *leaves*, and a matching upper bound for stratified monotone calibration is stated as open in Limitations. The connection is testable, and we tested it: our HKRR-style per-cell baseline is an empirical instantiation of exactly the class F_n the theorem lower-bounds — and it loses to CalFuse on 3 of 7 subsets (verified at 20 splits: nfcorpus p<0.0001, fiqa p=0.017, touche p=0.002) and never wins. The theorem predicts per-cell audits are worst-cell sample-starved at these calibration sizes; the data agrees. A "What Theorem 1 does and does not say" paragraph will make sure no reader infers more — or less.

**Q2. "Full-coverage NDCG unchanged, so where is the end-to-end gain?"**

Unchanged full-coverage NDCG is the *expected* outcome: post-hoc calibration is near-monotone and should not reorder rankings — a method that improved NDCG here would be doing something other than calibration. The deployment value sits at the decision layer of a thresholded system: (a) a new threshold-reliability experiment (20 splits × 7 subsets) shows that at fixed τ, the worst-stratum gap between realized precision and stated probability shrinks from 0.075 (Linear-Learned) to 0.036 (CalFuse) on nfcorpus and 0.170→0.067 on scidocs (both p<0.0001), never significantly worsens, while RRF's realized precision at the same τ varies by up to 0.80 across strata — a fixed threshold is simply not one policy for an uncalibrated fusion; (b) selective operation improves: on scifact, abstaining on the bottom half of calibrated scores cuts hallucination-among-answered from 30.8% to 15.2%; (c) at full coverage, the scifact hallucination reduction strengthened to 10 seeds during the discussion period (−3.49 pp vs the strongest learned baseline, p=0.0025, d=−1.31), with a mechanism test attributing it to distractor swap. We are transparent that (c) is subset-dependent at 7B and vanishes at Qwen-14B, and the revision scopes it accordingly: calibration makes the *thresholding* of retrieval trustworthy uniformly over subgroups; it reaches generation only where the generator is sensitive to context composition.
