We thank the AC and all three reviewers for careful, constructive reviews. This comment summarizes the cross-cutting concerns and the new experiments; per-reviewer replies contain details.

**1. Self-containedness and jargon (AC, Reviewer utWJ).** We agree the submission leaned too hard on unexpanded shorthand. The revision will make the paper self-contained: (i) Platt scaling defined at first use (a two-parameter logistic map p = σ(a·s+b) fit on held-out calibration data; Platt 1999, reference added); (ii) IVAP (Inductive Venn–Abers Predictor) defined in one paragraph before Section 4 — a nonparametric calibrator that fits isotonic regression twice on the calibration set (once appending the test point with label 0, once with label 1), yielding an interval [p_lo, p_hi] whose validity holds distribution-free under exchangeability (Vovk & Petej 2014); (iii) a baseline glossary table defining every method at first mention (single-retriever+Platt, RRF+Platt, Linear-Learned = logistic regression over per-signal calibrated logits, Subgroup-Platt / Subgroup-Isotonic = per-stratum recalibration of the fused score); (iv) "distractor swap" defined where it first appears: at fixed rank-of-first-positive, better-calibrated fusion changes *which non-relevant passages* fill the remaining top-k slots, with the mechanism test presented alongside the definition; (v) a notation table (τ, ECE-15, worst-subgroup ECE, stratum g, K cells, α) in Section 2.

**2. Role of the theory (all three reviewers).** We will add an explicit paragraph, "What Theorem 1 does and does not say." It *does* say: any post-hoc calibrator in the class F_n — methods whose per-cell output depends on calibration data only through per-cell empirical means (per-cell recalibration, HKRR-style audits, binned Subgroup-Platt) — must pay Θ(α⁻² log K) calibration samples in the worst cell, matching HKRR's upper bound, so within F_n the per-cell route is optimally sample-hungry. It does *not* say anything about CalFuse's own sample complexity: CalFuse is deliberately outside F_n (isotonic pooling shares strength across cells within each stratum), and the theorem is the reason to leave the class, not a guarantee about the method that leaves it. CalFuse's direct guarantees are of a different type — distribution-free per-stratum coverage of the Venn–Abers envelope under exchangeability, and multicalibration preservation of the parametric fusion stage under conditional independence. A matching upper bound for stratified monotone calibration is open; the revision says so in Limitations.

**3. New experiments run during the discussion phase** (all reproducible; calibration-side analyses recompute from the frozen score matrices in the released supplementary):

- **Seed-scaling of the core claims to 20 reseeded splits** (4× the submitted protocol). The headline survives and sharpens: CalFuse has the lowest worst-subgroup ECE-15 on 6 of 7 subsets and is statistically tied for lowest on the seventh; vs the strongest learned baseline, three wins at p≤0.002, four ties, no losses.
- **New threshold-reliability experiment** (20 splits): at fixed τ, the worst-stratum gap between realized precision and stated probability shrinks from 0.075 (Linear-Learned) to 0.036 (CalFuse) on nfcorpus and 0.170→0.067 on scidocs (both p<0.0001), never significantly worsens anywhere; RRF's realized precision at the same τ varies by up to 0.80 across strata (reply to utWJ, Q1).

The revision reports both and scopes the end-to-end claim accordingly (replies to utWJ Q4, T3MF Q2).
- **Empirical confirmation of Theorem 1's prediction**: the HKRR-style per-cell baseline (an instantiation of F_n) loses to CalFuse on 3 of 7 subsets and never wins (replies to T3MF, VkVU).
- **Query-bootstrap CIs for the selective-NDCG signature**: suggestive on trec-covid, absent on nfcorpus; claim downgraded accordingly (reply to T3MF, Q3).
