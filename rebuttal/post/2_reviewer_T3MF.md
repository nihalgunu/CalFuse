**Q1. How should the lower bound be interpreted relative to CalFuse?**

Your reading is correct and we will make it the paper's official one. Theorem 1 lower-bounds worst-cell sample complexity at Θ(α⁻² log K) for the class F_n of post-hoc calibrators whose per-cell output depends on calibration data only through per-cell empirical means; matching HKRR's upper bound, it shows the per-cell route is *optimally* sample-hungry within that class. It is therefore a design justification — the reason CalFuse pools strength across cells via per-stratum isotonic structure — not a performance guarantee for CalFuse, whose direct guarantees are of a different type (distribution-free per-stratum envelope coverage under exchangeability; multicalibration preservation of the fusion stage under conditional independence).

New evidence from the discussion period tightens this link: our HKRR-style per-cell baseline is precisely an empirical instantiation of F_n — and on worst-subgroup ECE-15 it loses to CalFuse on 3 of 7 subsets and never wins, verified at 20 reseeded splits (nfcorpus p<0.0001, fiqa p=0.017, touche p=0.002). The theorem's prediction is visible in the data. What remains missing — now stated as an open problem in Limitations — is a matching *upper* bound for stratified monotone calibration. We will retitle the theory section "Why per-cell post-hoc calibration cannot suffice" so no reader mistakes the bound for a guarantee about CalFuse.

**Q2. What can and cannot be concluded from the hallucination experiment?**

We extended it on both axes during the discussion period. (a) *Power:* doubled to 10 seeds on all five subsets. The scifact effect strengthens decisively (CalFuse-P vs Linear-Learned: −3.49 pp, p=0.0025, d=−1.31) — no longer statistically thin. A significant *deficit* also emerges for the parametric base on nfcorpus (+2.8 pp, p=0.014), which the full CalFuse repairs to parity (p=0.96); the rest remain inconclusive. (b) *Model scale:* on Qwen-2.5-14B (5 seeds, scifact/fiqa/nfcorpus), every between-method difference collapses (|Δ| ≤ 0.6 pp, n.s.) while overall hallucination drops (~34%→~23% on scifact).

Conclusions: for a 7B-class generator on a contested-composition subset, improving subgroup calibration changes *which* passages enter the context (mechanism test: rank-of-first-positive unchanged) and reduces hallucination robustly; at 14B the generator absorbs distractor differences and the end-to-end effect vanishes. The revision presents the experiment as a mechanism demonstration with an empirically mapped scope boundary (generator scale × subset composition), not a general claim — and we think the mapped boundary is itself informative.
